# Queen Mary 2 — the aft-quarter plates (r161)

The r132 residual: "her aft terraces are six near-identical full-width steps where the real
ship has fewer and more varied ones. Those u-spans came off the Commons scale DRAWING, and
the drawing is now the weak link — that wants a photographic aft-quarter plate." This file
pins the plates that hunt found, with each plate's scale and what precision it supports
(the r96 rule). Copies of the four plates used are committed beside this round's staging in
`build/staging/r161/`.

## The reads

**Read 1 — the terraces are laid decking, at both ends of the era. This read is settled and
carried into the record this round.** On plate 1 every aft terrace — the fantail with its
pool and jacuzzi, the short café step above it, the deep main-pool terrace, the shallow
awning step, the shallow jacuzzi step — reads a warm plank field, metres wide, clearly
distinct from the white coated structure around it. On plates 2 and 2b (July 2004, as
built) the same terraces read the same warm field around the same pools. The r108
`roofsProvenance` sentence "her aft terraces are pale coated plate in every photograph" is
refuted by both dates; the record's own `deck.provenance` already said "photographs of her
terraced stern show laid decking there." The crest is NOT among the covered surfaces: the
topmost block's roof (dome and mast base, plate 1) and the large shell roof forward of the
cascade read white coated plate at both dates. That is exactly the r159 word: `roofs:
'terraces'` — covering on every exposed tier roof except the top tier's.

**Read 2 — the cascade is five terraces with the deep ones low and mid, not seven near-equal
steps. This read is structural; the numbers need the measuring round.** From the counter
up, plate 1 shows: (A) the long rounded fantail carrying a pool and jacuzzi, the deepest
step of all; (B) a short café step; (C) the deep main-pool terrace, the second-deepest;
(D) a shallow step with awnings at its aft edge; (E) a shallow step (post-refit it carries
a round elevated jacuzzi); then the crest block. The model's seven drawn steps put its
deepest terrace near the top of the cascade; the ship has her two deep terraces at the
bottom and the middle. Plate 2 shows the same five-level cascade as built, with one
difference at the top: the 2016 remastering replaced the deck-13 aft splash pool and
Regatta Bar with a block of 30 cabins (Cunard, May 27 – June 21 2016, Blohm+Voss; stated
at avidcruiser.com 2016-01 and beyondcruise.com "Queen Mary 2 Remastered update"), so the
topmost step is open terrace as built and cabin front after 2016. Every level below deck
13 is unchanged by the refit.

**Read 3 — two geometry classes the model does not draw, named as residuals.** (a) The side
balcony wings run aft PAST each terrace floor, so a terrace sits recessed between two wings;
the model ends the whole tier at one u. (b) Each terrace's aft rail is curved in plan,
following the stern's round, and the fantail rail carries a row of outward-leaning glass
windscreen panels (plates 1 and 3); the model cuts every tier square across.

## What the measuring round (r162) owes

Re-derive `tierAftU` off plate 1: for each of the five risers, the u of the enclosed
structure's aft face, scaled per level by counting 2.6 m balcony bays on the adjacent wing
(integer counts, so the error is a stated ±half-bay per edge, ±1.3 m). Adjacent model tiers
that share one real riser take the same u — that is what makes five steps out of eight
tiers. The level-to-tier mapping runs on the model's own tier floors (23.4 m + 2.95 m per
tier against the cascade heights in plate 2), not on Cunard deck names. Decide there
whether the drawn form keeps the as-built open top step (the record's year is 2003) and
say so in the provenance either way.
