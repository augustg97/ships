# Salamis — the record behind the campaign data (round 80)

The second staged campaign, and the first galley action. Unblocked since round 50, when the
battle stopped keeping its own speed model: the trireme's polar carries the Olympias oar floor
(5.4 kn cruise, −0.31 kn per m/s of headwind, floor 2.9 kn measured into a head sea), so a
fleet of them sails here at true scale with no engine work at all. What this file records is
the other half: the campaign's days, winds, ranges and text, each tied to its source, and the
modeling decisions where the view's own conventions apply.

## Sources read this round, verbatim

**Herodotus VIII (Rawlinson), fetched from the Internet Classics Archive:**
- 8.60, Themistocles to Eurybiades — the tactical argument the whole battle turns on:
  *"to fight in a narrow space is favourable to us — in an open sea, to them."*
- 8.48, the Greek fleet: *"The whole number of the ships, without counting the penteconters,
  was three hundred and seventy-eight."* (His own city-by-city list sums to 366 — the classic
  internal discrepancy; Aeschylus, who fought, says 310. CONTESTED, carried on the card.)
- 8.76, the night move: *"they advanced their western wing towards Salamis, so as to inclose
  the Greeks"* … *"The Persian troops were landed on the islet of Psyttaleia."*
- 8.89, why the losses ran so unequal: *"more perished by drowning than in any other way,
  since they did not know how to swim."*

**Plutarch, Themistocles 14 (Dryden), fetched from the Internet Classics Archive** — the wind
record, and the reason the campaign's fighting phases carry force 4 from the south:
> "he would not run the prows of his galleys against the Persians, nor begin the fight till
> the time of day was come, when there regularly blows in a fresh breeze from the open sea,
> and brings in with it a strong swell into the channel; which was no inconvenience to the
> Greek ships, which were low-built, and little above the water, but did much to hurt the
> Persians, which had high sterns and lofty decks, and were heavy and cumbrous in their
> movements"

Also Plutarch on the flagship duel: Ariamenes, "admiral to Xerxes … throwing darts and
shooting arrows from his huge galley, as from the walls of a castle", boarded by Ameinias'
crew "upon the ships meeting stem to stem" and thrust into the sea; Artemisia knew his
floating body and carried it to Xerxes. (Herodotus 8.89 calls the king's brother who died
ARIABIGNES; the name is carried as Herodotus has it, with Plutarch's variant noted here.)

**Aeschylus, Persae (Perseus, Greek text)** — the messenger's speech, the only surviving
eyewitness account (Aeschylus fought in the battle):
- 382–384, the all-night rowing: *καὶ πάννυχοι δὴ διάπλοον καθίστασαν / ναῶν ἄνακτες πάντα
  ναυτικὸν λεών* — the squadron commanders kept the whole ships' companies crossing to and
  fro all night.
- 388–391, first light: *πρῶτον μὲν ἠχῇ κέλαδος Ἑλλήνων πάρα / μολπηδὸν ηὐφήμησεν, ὄρθιον δ᾽
  ἅμα / ἀντηλάλαξε νησιώτιδος πέτρας / ἠχώ* — the Greek battle-song first, and the echo of it
  back off the island rocks.
- 419–421 (from the standard text; the fetched page cut off before it): the sea *no longer
  visible, filled with wrecks and the slaughter of men* — the rout phase's picture.
- 338–343: Aeschylus' own numbers — 310 Greek ships against 1,207, of which 207 fast.

## The campaign's nine phases, each tied to its evidence

1. **The week before** — Athens evacuated, the Acropolis burned (Hdt 8.51–53); the allied
   fleet in the bays of Salamis town, the Persian fleet beached at Phalerum (8.66–67).
2. **The council, at night** — the Peloponnesians vote for the Isthmus; Themistocles' narrows
   argument (8.60, quoted above); Eurybiades holds the fleet (8.56–63).
3. **Dusk: Sicinnus** — Themistocles' slave rowed to the Persian commanders with the message
   that the Greeks mean to slip away (Hdt 8.75; Persae 355–360 has the same "deceiving Greek
   man"). Xerxes orders the exits closed.
4. **Night: the encirclement** — the western wing advanced to enclose (8.76), troops onto
   Psyttaleia, the crews at the oar all night (Persae 382–4).
5. **Dawn** — Aristides through the blockade from Aegina with the news they are surrounded
   (8.79–81); the paean and its echo (Persae 388–91); the Persian column comes on into the
   narrows.
6. **Mid-morning: the crush** — Themistocles waits for the breeze and its swell (Plut. Them.
   14); Ameinias of Pallene rams first (Hdt 8.84); the rear lines pressing up foul their own
   van (8.89); Artemisia escapes by ramming a ship of her own side (8.87–88).
7. **Afternoon: the rout** — the sea carpeted with wrecks (Persae 419–21); the drownings
   (8.89); Ariabignes dead; Aristides lands hoplites on Psyttaleia and kills the garrison
   (8.95).
8. **Evening** — the survivors run for Phalerum under the army's cover; the Aeginetans ambush
   the channel out (8.91–92); Xerxes watched the whole day from the foot of Mt. Aigaleos
   (8.90); losses per Diodorus.
9. **The days after** — the fleet ordered to the Hellespont (8.107); Mardonius winters in
   Thessaly for Plataea; the invasion's naval arm is finished.

## Modeling decisions, stated

- **Both fleets are the trireme hull.** That is the record: the Persian fleet at Salamis was
  triremes — Phoenician, Egyptian, Ionian — the same type the Greeks rowed, taller in the
  stern and higher-decked per Plutarch but the same class of ship. The sides are told apart
  by formation (an ordered double line against a crowded triple column) and by the card, not
  by inventing a different hull.
- **Canvas stowed.** A trireme fought under oar; the masts were struck or left ashore before
  action. The model's nearest state is `furled: true` — bare spars are drawn where the
  reality was often no mast at all. A known simplification, same class as the r79 rope-for-
  chain note.
- **No powder.** `powder: false` on the battle record: 480 BC has no guns, so the action
  phases draw no broadside smoke. The smoke path now asks the record instead of assuming
  1588.
- **Night winds are the model's own.** The sources give the daily southerly breeze (Plutarch)
  and nothing for the small hours; the campaign holds the wind axis S–SSE at force 1–2
  through the night so the enemy bearing does not wander, and force 4 for the fighting
  phases, which is what "a fresh breeze … a strong swell into the channel" reads as.
- **Positions are board positions.** Same convention as the Armada campaign (battle.js's own
  note: offsets "drawn for legibility at ocean scale"); the true engagement range is `rng`,
  from 9 km (Phalerum to the strait) down to 130 m at the crush — Herodotus' narrow space,
  visible as distance, exactly as Gravelines' 110 m is.
- **The strait's shores are NOT drawn.** The action view is open water; at Salamis the land
  (Kynosoura, Psyttaleia, Aigaleos) stood inside the view's 3.4 km haze and framed the whole
  fight. That is a real gap of this round, carried openly in HANDOFF rather than patched
  with a cardboard ridge that would break rule 0(a).
