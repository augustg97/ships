# Card tone — the rule

August, on reading the fleet: the cards are *"a bit too casual, over dramatic or cliché"* and in
places *"too meta"*. The standard is **Wikipedia**: objective, descriptive, clear. We are showing
the story of ships, not arguing for it.

## What to cut

**Announcing significance instead of stating the fact.** If something matters, the fact carries
it. Say what happened; do not tell the reader it is the point.

> ✗ "Small, shallow and weatherly — and the weatherliness is the point."
> ✓ "A small, shallow, weatherly hull. Its significance is its ability to sail to windward:
>    a cog could run down to Guinea on the trades but could not return against them."

**Editorial verdicts.** "Underrated", "the fact worth stopping on", "the whole argument",
"which is the point of it at all". These rank and persuade; the reader can do that.

> ✗ "The battened sail is the underrated one."
> ✓ "The battens make the sail self-reefing and allow it to set flat."

**Meta commentary — the worst class.** A card is about a ship. The moment it discusses the
model's own editorial choices it has stepped outside the frame.

> ✗ "…and because this is a model about technology, that belongs on a vessel card rather than
>    in a note."
> ✓ "For three centuries the principal cargo carried across the Atlantic was people. An
>    estimated 12.5 million were embarked and 10.7 million landed."

**Rhetorical rhythm** — the short declarative sentence deployed for effect, the em-dash reveal,
the one-line paragraph. Occasionally fine; as a habit it reads as a house style rather than as
description.

## What to keep

Precision, and contested figures shown as contested. Naming an uncertainty is descriptive, not
editorial: *"Ships lost: 35 (Casado Soto) – 63 (Fernández Duro)"* is exactly right.

Concrete numbers over adjectives. "One man per 2.6 tons against one per 20–30 in a merchantman"
needs no adverb.

## Scope

Applies to vessel `text`, card `rows`, rig notes, part descriptions in `PARTS`, and the
Shipwright's stage descriptions. **Not** to code comments — those are working notes for whoever
edits the file next, and a different register is correct there.
