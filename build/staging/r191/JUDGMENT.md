(松井広信「四爪鉄錨の基礎的研究」金沢大学考古学紀要 34, 2013, 27–52) carries 表1 on p.38 —
the measurement table for all 49 corpus anchors — and it was never read at member level.
r187 took from it only 全長 and one ring (Onominato a). Read whole today (page rendered at
300 DPI, both halves, transcribed):

- **Arm sections, per anchor, rectangular**: Arm 最大幅 / 最大厚 and Arm 最少幅 / 最少厚
  (フルーク先端) — width AND thickness, i.e. the arms are flat forged bars, not round.
  For the 27 anchors ≥ 190 cm whose values print consistently (rows 41, 42 excluded —
  their printed 最少幅 exceeds their 最大幅):
  - arm root: **0.0346 ± 0.0037 wide × 0.0198 ± 0.0033 thick** (fractions of 全長), W:T ≈ 1.8
  - fluke tip: **0.0220 ± 0.0052 × 0.0058 ± 0.0031** — a wide thin blade
- **Ring bars measured**: AR c (幅) ~0.9–3 cm; AcR b×c ~2–4.5 cm. The r187 drawn ring bar

## The judgment

1. **Mass field**: sekibune `yotsumeAnchor.kg = 122` — DERIVED, cube-law from the one
   weighed find (335 kg at 2.8 m → 122.1 at the class-default 2.0 m). The card's prose
   "about 120 kg" becomes an audited property.
2. **Solve (the r190 pattern)**: recorded members are not knobs — arms and rings are drawn
   at the record's own sections. The shank is the ONE member 表1 does not record; it takes
   the mass budget: its square side is solved so the drawn iron weighs the record's kg at
   7850 kg/m³. At (2.0 m, 122 kg) the solve returns side 9.4 cm at the crown tapering to
   6.8 (0.0471·全長), and by cube-law the same fraction at any similar (len, kg).
3. **Form fixes carried by the same read**: arms become rectangular bars (4-segment
   cylinders, faces axis-aligned, per-piece thickness scale), tip a flat blade cone; head
   ring takes the type anchor's own teardrop proportions (semi 0.0818 × 0.0353, bar half
   0.0056, elongation 2.32); accessory ring takes its measured section (R 0.0590, tube
   0.0079).
4. **NEW RULE V-YMASS**: iron integrated from the built scene — analytic member volumes
   (square-bar frustum for 4-segment cylinders, blade cone, torus) through each world
   matrix's determinant — vs the record's kg, 12% band. When the record carries no kg the
   rule audits the same cube-law default the builder draws by (both sides share one
   derivation, named DERIVED in the provenance), so the rule is never vacuous; it passes
   over an anchor with missing members (V-YARMS/V-YRING's fault).

## What is contested, named

The paper's own monument photographs read the shank at **0.023–0.034 of 全長** (fig 15-1①,
Fukuura #15, 259 cm: masked-width read 5–10 cm along the run; obliquity unbounded, so these
are upper bounds per station) — the solve wants 0.0471. The weighed record and the
photographed monuments disagree at the ~30–50% level on the shank. Named suspects: a
century of outdoor rust exfoliation on the monuments (保存状況はよくない — the table's
calipers measure today's survivors), the forklift's own precision, and real spread between
working wreck tackle and votive survivors. Under the standing rule the weighed record beats
my derivation from a garden photograph. **Residual: a caliper record of the Kozushima
anchor's members would settle the shank; fetch the 國學院大學 1993 site report
(神津島沖海底遺跡) if it surfaces open.**
