# r182 — The anchors line opens: the Belitung composite grapnel, DRAWN

## The warrant

DIRECT — the strongest any ground tackle in the fleet carries. The drawn
instance's own wreck preserved the anchor. No extension, no tradition-read,
no gear-list of another ship: the object itself was excavated at the site.

The windlass line (r172–r179, eight hulls) set the precedent structure this
round applies: judgment first, warrant named, every dimension sourced or
named as default, silence honoured where the record is silent. Here the
record is not silent — it is a photographed, drawn, published object.

## Sources verified this round

1. **Flecker, World Archaeology 32:3 (2001)** — local from r178, re-read.
   "the ship's nautical equipment may be represented by a composite
   grapnel-type anchor, the shank of wood and the arms of iron, found at
   the site (Plate 11)". The hedge ("may be represented by") is the
   author's and is carried into the provenance. Plate 11: "The composite
   iron and wood anchor sitting upright on the seabed." Timber: "The
   frames and the anchor shank were either Afzelia sp. ... or Amoora sp."
   Harari comparison: "an iron grapnel with a wooden shank ... the Harari
   anchor has two arms that cross each other at different levels."

2. **Flecker, in Krahl/Guy/Wilson/Raby, *Shipwrecked: Tang Treasures and
   Monsoon Winds* (Smithsonian 2010), pp. 112–13, figs. 84–85** — FETCHED
   WHOLE this round (archive.asia.si.edu/Shipwrecked/downloads/07Flecker.pdf,
   the open Smithsonian copy; Vosmer's chapter 08 fetched too — zero anchor
   mentions, checked). The full form: "The arms protrude straight outward
   and are separated vertically by a heavy iron bell-shaped disk, which has
   a hole through the center. ... The disk is a form that lends itself to
   casting, while the arms are thought to be wrought iron." Only the lower
   end of the wooden shank survived; one arm broken. Ibn Majid: al-hadid
   ("the iron") an iron grapnel; al-anjar al-siniya "the Chinese anchor";
   cast iron was China's alone in the 9th c. — the disk may be Chinese-cast.

## What is drawn, and at what standing

- Arms, bell, shank thicknesses, the two-level cross: RECORDED (fig. 84 at
  its own scale bar).
- The broken pair completed at the intact pair's 1.72 m span: INFERENCE,
  named.
- Shank length 1.8 m: RECONSTRUCTION, named — preserved nowhere, about the
  arm span over again; no fetched source preserves or reconstructs it.
- Stow (loose on the foredeck, u 0.125, shank laid aft and yawed past the
  foremast's step, coir cable and coil): PLACEMENT INFERENCE, named — the
  tradition's documented practice read back, no source places the anchor
  aboard.
- NOT drawn: a kalba cat-beam (the tradition's other stow — attested at its
  documented END, not for the 9th c., and the drawn hull has none); a
  hawse or any winding machine (r178's silence stands).

## The class, not the instance

- Builder: generic `grapnel` record block in buildFittings (hull.js), datum-
  driven like the windlass — any future hull whose record attests a grapnel
  draws one from its own dimensions. Silence draws nothing.
- Audit (audit-hulls.js, new rule): V-WARRANT both ways (meshes with no
  record convict; a record with no meshes convicts), V-ARMS (four tips,
  found structurally as spheres), V-SPAN (greatest pairwise tip distance
  reads the record's span — a box read cannot, the stow is spun 45°),
  V-REST (lowest drawn point on the deck at its own station, asked of the
  surface: neither floating nor through the planking).
- PARTS gains `grapnel` (stage 3) with its own card text — the bower-anchor
  card no longer has to lie for the one anchor that is not a bower.
