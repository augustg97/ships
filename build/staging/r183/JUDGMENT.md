# r183 — the junk's anchor: judged before drawn

## The question

The r182 residual 1 names the anchors line's next members from r174: "the junk's and
treasure-ship's attested anchors (Xu Jing's stone anchor with wooden flukes, the TGK's
five or six iron anchors)". Scope decision for this round: the JUNK alone, done
properly. Her warrant is the stronger of the two — an eyewitness of her own class and
century — and her anchor is the r174 windlass's own load, completing a machine already
drawn. The treasure-ship's iron anchors stay at the head of the queue, and their round
owes a fetch this file names below.

## The warrant — DIRECT (eyewitness text, the drawn hull's own class, era and route)

Xu Jing, Xuanhe fengshi Gaoli tujing (1124), 卷34 — fetched whole in r174, local at
build/staging/r174/gaoli-tujing-34.txt, line 11, the SAME SENTENCE that drew the
windlass:

> 船首兩頰柱中，有車輪，上綰藤索，其大如椽，長五百尺。下垂矴石，石兩旁，夾以二木鈎。
> 船未入洋，近山抛泊，則放矴箸水底，如維纜之屬，舟乃不行。若風濤緊急，則加游矴，
> 其用如大矴，而在其兩旁。遇行則卷其輪，而收之。

"At the ship's head between the two cheek-posts there is a wheel, winding a rattan
cable thick as a rafter, five hundred feet long. Below HANGS THE ANCHOR-STONE; on the
stone's two sides it is CLAMPED BY TWO WOODEN HOOKS. Before the ship enters the open
ocean, anchoring near the islands, they let the anchor down to the sea floor and the
ship holds. If wind and sea turn urgent they add the roving anchors (游矴), used like
the great anchor, carried at its two sides. Getting under way, they wind the wheel and
recover it."

The ship this stands on is the passage's own 客舟 — 長十餘丈 (line 11, "ten-odd zhang
long"), the drawn junk's own size (34 m), class (sea trader) and century. The r174
judgment already accepted this witness for the windlass at DIRECT warrant; the anchor
is the same sentence's object.

## The form — the text's, and nothing more

The text gives: a stone (the weight), two wooden hooks clamping it one to each side
(the flukes), a rattan cable, and the winch that lifts it. No shank is named, no iron
anywhere. Drawn exactly so: a shaped stone bar hanging vertically on the cable, two
hardwood hooks lashed along its two sides, their points curving outward past the
stone's foot to bite whichever way it lands, rattan bands clamping the bundle.

Corroboration in the region's material record, already inside this fleet's own
provenances: the Korean institute's component classification of the excavated Goryeo
ships lists anchoring gear as 닻 (anchor), 호롱 (windlass), 닻돌 (ANCHOR STONES), 엄말
(read r175, in the panokseon's windlassProvenance) — stone-weighted wooden anchors are
the East Asian tradition's class, not this text's invention.

## Every dimension, named

- **Stone bar 1.6 × 0.24 × 0.19 m** — CLASS DEFAULT, no fetched source carries a
  number. About 0.07 m³, roughly 190 kg in granite — a load the r174 windlass's
  four-man barrel can break out. NOT FETCHED, named: the Quanzhou-bay and Hakata-bay
  碇石 finds (dimensioned publications are in Chinese/Japanese archaeology journals
  this round could not reach; a ja.wikipedia title search returned no dimensioned
  article), the Shinan wreck's anchoring-gear report. Any of them, fetched with a
  measured bar, replaces this default.
- **Hooks 2.1 m of wood each, 0.12 m diameter** — CLASS DEFAULT sized to the stone
  they clamp (shaft the stone's length plus the outward curve at the foot).
- **Cable diameter 0.10 m** — INFERENCE from the text's own simile, 其大如椽 ("thick
  as a rafter"); length 500 尺 (~155 m) is the record's, not drawn whole.
- **Hang and stow** — PLACEMENT INFERENCE, named in the provenance. The text's own
  verbs: 下垂 (hangs below) in use, 卷其輪而收之 (wound up and recovered) under way.
  The drawn ship is under sail, so the anchor rides recovered: hung at the prow close
  under the stem head, its cable led back over the bow to the windlass barrel — the
  lead over the stem is a drawn simplification (no hawse hole is attested or drawn).
  The 游矴 spare anchors are NOT drawn: the text carries them 在其兩旁 ("at its two
  sides") in use, and no stowed position is attested — drawing a stow would invent
  place, the r172 class of error.

## The class, not the instance

- Generic datum-driven `stoneAnchor` block in buildFittings — any hull whose record
  attests one draws it from its own dimensions; silence draws nothing.
- Its own PARTS card (the grapnel card is another ocean's tradition; the bower card
  is iron with a stock — neither may cover for this object).
- NEW audit rule, generic over `H.stoneAnchor`: V-WARRANT both ways; V-STONE (one
  stone bar, longest drawn dimension the record's stoneLenM); V-HOOKS (two points,
  found structurally as the group's cones); V-HANG (every drawn point of the hanging
  assembly above the water and its head below the stem-head sheer — a stone dragging
  in the sea and a stone floating over the bow both convict); V-CABLE (the text's one
  sentence carries wheel, cable and stone together — a drawn stone with no cable
  convicts).

## Owed by the NEXT round (treasure-ship, staying at the queue head)

iron anchors per ship, the 500-catty 看家錨, two worked at the head, two at the stern,

transport's 器具皆同 carries the gear to sea; the treasure fleet is ~200 years
