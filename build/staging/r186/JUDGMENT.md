## What each source can support — and what it cannot

1. **Presence — her own navy's album.** encykorea E0000455 (각선도본, c. 1797, the
   plate r175 pinned): the 전선 warship plate draws "돛대·닻［碇］·노(櫓)·키 등
   부속품도 잘 그려져 있고" — mast, ANCHOR, oars, rudder, well drawn. Two things this
   sentence carries: the anchor is ON the warship plate (r175 could only cite the
   plate's list; the windlass was not in it, the anchor was), and the encyclopedia
   glosses 닻 with the character 碇 — the STONE-anchor character, the same one Xu
   Jing's 矴石 and the Korean 정(碇) tradition carry. What the sentence cannot
   support: the drawn anchor's FORM. No open image of the plate was found this round
   (searched; the album is Kyujanggak's — 2018 conservation report s-space.snu.ac.kr
   10371/164202 names it; NOT fetched). A blog-level summary (search excerpt, kmhistory)
   calls the 전선도 the depiction of the 천자일호좌선, the fleet commander's own
   panokseon-class ship, and the basic design record for panokseon research — carrying
   page named, not load-bearing.

2. **The tradition's composite form — the West Sea's own record.** KHS press release
   (khs.go.kr newsItemId 155704520, fetched whole): the institute recovered **154
   anchor stones** from the central West Sea 2008–2019 — Mado 141, Seomeombeol 9,
   Daeseom 2, Dangampo and Kkotseom 1 each — and radiocarbon on the **wooden anchors
   and fiber ropes found WITH them** runs "기원전 2세기부터 삼국시대, 고려~조선시대까지"
   — 2nd century BC through Three Kingdoms, Goryeo and JOSEON. The panokseon's own
   dynasty is inside the recorded range, on her navy's own coast. The exhibition of the
   same finds (m-i.kr 844666): stones are natural rock roughly worked **rectangular**,
   with **rope grooves cut "나무닻과 묶을 수 있도록"** — to lash to the wooden anchor;
   the recorded size class runs "길이 40~50cm, 5~30kg 소형부터 **길이 2m가 넘고
   300~700kg에 이르는 대형**" — small 40–50 cm at 5–30 kg up to large stones over 2 m
   at 300–700 kg. A reproduction showing HOW wood and stone combine exists in that
   exhibition — named here, NOT fetched (no drawing or photo of it found).

3. **The wooden anchor's own parts — the dictionary is the authority fetched.**
   표준국어대사전: **닻가지** "가지처럼 벋은, 닻에 달린 갈고리. **보통 네 갈고리이며
   두 갈고리도 있다**" — hook-arms spreading like branches, USUALLY FOUR, two also
   occur. **닻장** "닻채 **위에** 닻줄을 매도록 **가로 박은** 나무나 쇠막대기" — a bar
   fixed crosswise through the upper shank, where the cable bends. Tradition summaries
   (culturecontent search excerpt): "닻은 기둥 같이 생긴 몸통인 **닻채**와 이 닻채의
   **양쪽에** 달아진 갈고리 가지로 구성" — a post-like shank with the hook-arms hung
   on BOTH SIDES; "한국의 닻은 **참나무로 크게** 만들어서 갯벌에 깊게 박혀 걸리게 한
   것이 특징" — characteristically LARGE, of oak, to bury deep in tidal mud.

4. **The living builder.** 마광남, 배목수이야기 42 (hanion.co.kr 20724, fetched): the
   石碇/木碇/鐵碇 taxonomy; wood regionally 가시나무 (Wando evergreen oak) or 참나무
   (N. Jeolla oak), chosen because "나무 자체가 워낙 무거워서 … 쉽게 가라앉기" — heavy,
   sinking, rot-resistant; and against soak-buoyancy "**닻장에 작은 돌들을 묶어서**
   사용하였다" — stones bound on; his own figure of the Chinese cognate: "큰 돌을
   깎아서 닻장을 만들어 사용" — the Chinese carve the big stone AS the cross-member.
   He also records a trip-ring on an arm with its own line and buoy — a fishing
   practice with no warship warrant; NOT drawn, named here.

5. **Local since r175:** the institute's gear classification of the excavated carriers,

## The judgment: DRAW, warrant NAVY-TRADITION EXTENSION — presence one step stronger than r175's

The warrant is r175's own shape with the presence leg DIRECT-BY-ALBUM: the machine
round had to reach the grain-ship plate for its horong; the ANCHOR is named on the
전선 warship plate itself, the plate held to depict the fleet commander's
panokseon-class ship. Form is the tradition's: the West-Sea composite — oak shank,
hook-arms both sides, crossbar at the head, a long grooved rectangular stone lashed
on to sink it — whose recorded run (2nd c. BC → Joseon, on her own coast, radiocarbon
on the wooden anchors themselves) brackets her. No fetched source draws a
16th-century warship's anchor directly; the provenance says exactly that.

## The change — class, not instance

`woodAnchor: {atU 0.045, offZ 1.35, shankM 3.2, armM 1.2, arms 4, crossM 1.6,
stoneLenM 2.0, stoneSecM 0.30, cableDiaM 0.10}` + `anchorProvenance` on the
panokseon. A generic datum-driven `woodAnchor` block in buildFittings — silence
draws nothing; only a woodAnchor record draws one. Every dimension's status:

- **arms 4** — the dictionary's 보통 (usual) count, two hung each side of the shank;
  the two-hook variant is the dictionary's own alternative, named.
- **stone 2.0 × 0.30 × 0.25 m** — the RECORDED large class at its threshold ("길이
  2m가 넘고, 300~700kg"): granite at those dims ≈ 400 kg, inside the recorded band.
  A load the drawn four-man horong with its 2.0 m through-bars breaks out at ~8:1
  purchase (the r183 check, re-run for this weight).
- **stone lashed across the shank just above the crown, its long axis along the
  crossbar's** — a DRAWN INFERENCE, named: the placement follows the boatwright's own
  Chinese-cognate figure (the stone as cross-member) and the stone's recorded function
  (sink the wood, force the hooks down); the institute's reproduction shows the real
  lashing and REPLACES this the round it is fetched.
- **shank 3.2 m, arms 1.2 m, crossbar 1.6 m, sections 0.20/0.12/0.12** — DRAWN CLASS
  DEFAULTS proportioned to the stone and the 32 m hull, named as such; no fetched
  source dimensions the naval anchor (the Azzam rule — the only plates fetched carry
  no scale).
- **Stow** — PLACEMENT INFERENCE, the fleet rule (r182–r185): the drawn ship is under
  way, the anchor rides recovered on the open foredeck forward of its own horong,
  shank fore-and-aft with the arms forward, rolled 45° so no member stands vertical,
  pitched to the measured deck gradient, settled by box; the cable bent at the shank
  head above the 닻장 and led to the horong's barrel, where the machine holds the
  rest — no coil on deck, same as the junk (r183). Exact station/offZ iterated by
  measurement below.

## The audit rule — new, its own watcher, proven able to convict

The r182/r183/r184 pattern, keyed on the woodAnchor record: **V-WARRANT** both ways
(record with no meshes / meshes with no record); **V-ARMS** — the record's arm count
of tip cones found structurally in the group; **V-STONE** — the stone's longest
dimension read THROUGH the stow transform (sorted world-box extents, the r183 rule)
against stoneLenM; **V-CROSS** — the 닻장 is a record field no box can see: a
cross-member must sit within 0.35 m of the shank head with its axis athwart the
shank's; **V-REST** — the assembly rests on the deck ASKED OF THE SURFACE (ray
straight down from assembly centre, first non-anchor hit, the r185 form); **V-CABLE**
— a cable member exists and reaches the windlass barrel's station. Proofs before
ship: sever (builder strips the arms under a faithful record) must convict V-ARMS
and nothing about the stone; drag (stoneLenM 2.0 → 2.6 under the faithful builder)
must convict V-STONE with the transform-read number.
