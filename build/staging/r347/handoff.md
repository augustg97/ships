**THE FAULT.** `mastLowerOf` returned `height × (lwl + beam)/2` for a mast with no recorded height, and `buildRig` stood that length on the deck. The comment above it quotes Steel: "the length of the lower deck and extreme breadth being added together, the half is the length of the main-mast". That is the length in the mast tables, heel to head: 107 ft 6 in on this hull, against the 108 ft the tables give a 74's main mast. The heel steps on the keelson, so the depth in hold and the height of the gun deck are inside it.

**THE STEP DEPTH**, `hull.mastStep.belowDeckM` = 7.85 m. This is the depth in hold of a Bellona-class 74, 19 ft 9 in (6.02 m, attested: Wikipedia's Bellona and Dragon, 1760). To it are added 2.13 m from the lower gun deck to the upper deck (7 ft, a class figure, contested), and 0.30 m is taken off for the keelson's height over the ceiling (a class figure). The same depth is used at all three masts. On a real 74 the floors rise toward the ends, so the fore and mizzen steps stood somewhat higher. No source here says by how much.
