Round 327: the sekibune's mast no longer carries a European fighting top. Since round 67 the builder gave a top to every square lower mast dated 1100 or later, and the date was the only test; the sekibune, dated 1597, drew a walled round top with her yard hung under its trestletrees. The Japanese mast carried no top. Its head carried the semi, a sheave block the halyard runs over (Minamichita wasen museum), and her own plate, the Busan scroll of 1593, draws the yard slung at the head with about a metre of bare pole above it and no platform. The record now says top.form 'none', masthead 'semi', with its provenance; the builder draws no top on such a mast and draws the block at the head. r67's audit rule no longer requires a top on it, and A-MASTHEAD-NONE holds the mast to its record; proofs A, B, C and D find 2, 2, 1 and 1 problems. The audit finds 0 problems on 33 hulls. The frames are scored in the receipt.
