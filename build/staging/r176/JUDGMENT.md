## What each source can support — and what it cannot

1. **The machine itself** — 南知多町 和船文化 (Minamichita town wasen museum),
   minamichita-museum.com/wasen/ship.html, 船材 item 5 轆轤, verbatim:
   「船の後部の居住区である屋（矢）倉の中に二組装備され、帆の上げ下げ、伝馬船や碇の
   積み卸しなど重量物の取り扱いに使用したもの。帆桁や伝馬船、碇に取り付けられた綱は
   帆柱上端の蝉（滑車）を通り、船尾の艫滑車（とびくるま、飛蝉（とびせみ）ともいう）を
   介して轆轤（ろくろ）で巻き取られる仕組みになっていました。轆轤が船内に設置され、
   弁才船の乗組員の作業効率が格段に向上したと考えられています。」
   Two sets, INSIDE the aft yagura living quarters; line from the masthead 蝉 block over
   the stern 艫飛車 block; yard, tender and anchor all worked from within the ship. The
   subject throughout is the 弁才船 — the Edo cargo ship — and the last sentence treats
   the machine's installation aboard as a development within that type's history.
   Photographs saved to build/staging/r176/ and READ this round (form only, no dimension
   taken — the pages give no scale):
   - rokuro01.png (the surviving spindle): a long TAPERED spindle, square through-holes
     and round pins mid-body — lying on the museum floor.
   - rokuro02.png (Hōkyū-maru model, yagura interior): the spindle stands VERTICAL,
     foot on the lower deck, head against the beam above, two long bars passed through
     crosswise at different heights, rope coiled beside it.
   - rokuro04.png (full-size usage replica): the same, upright between floor and
     overhead beam; the rope comes in LOW and winds at the spindle's foot.
   **The wasen rokuro is a VERTICAL-AXIS machine.** The r175 residual note said "a
   different machine in a different place"; it is also a different axis. The audit's own
   V-AXIS arm (r173) says a vertical barrel is a capstan wearing the wrong name — so
   `windlass` cannot carry it; and Falconer's capstan (r172 buildCapstan: whelps,
   drumhead, pawls) is not it either.

2. **Second attestation, same tradition** — 船の科学館 ものしりシート No.28 (Museum of
   Maritime Science, via 日本財団図書館), nippon.zaidan.info/seikabutsu/2000/00200/contents/055.htm:
   「重い帆桁の上下も、帆柱の先端の蝉(せみ)とよばれる滑車を通して船尾に縄を通し、
   轆轤(ろくろ)と呼ばれる人力の巻き上げ機を使って船内から行いました。」 The subject is
   again the 弁才船. Same masthead block, same lead aft, same working from within.

3. **The class definition** — 和漢船用集 (1761, Kanazawa Kanemitsu, Osaka shipwright),
   quoted at kuregure.hatenablog.com/entry/2024/11/01/231715: 40+ oars WITH a yagura is
   a sekibune; the kiwari (design rules) patterned only AFTER the 1590s invasion. The
   quoted passages name no hoisting machine.

4. **The class's own record is nearly blank** — 村上海賊ミュージアム staff blog
   (suigun-staff.blogspot.com/2020/05/urkami-kaizoku9.html, 2020, leaning on 石井謙治
   『図説和船史話』1983 as the field's standard), verbatim: 「安宅船に対し、関船や後に
   紹介する小早船は、当時の文献や絵図ではほとんど登場しないので実態がよくわからない
   部分が多いのも事実です。」 The sekibune barely appears in contemporary documents or
   drawings at all.

5. **The identity gloss** — oceandictionary.jp 和船語彙集, capstan entry: 「ろくろのように
   回転軸が垂直で…」 — the dictionary explains the capstan BY the rokuro's vertical
   axis. Confirms 1's reading independently.

Not fetched: 石井謙治's books themselves (print only); the 難波船軍図 and 屏風 plates in
resolution (the suigun blog cannot reproduce the Nagoya-jō byōbu image). Named as such.

## The judgment: SILENT, and why

No fetched source puts a rokuro — or any winch — on a sekibune or any Sengoku warship.
The drawn ship is the 1597 Myeongnyang instance (hull.year 1597). Drawing the machine
would stack THREE inferences, each unsourced:

- **Era**: every attestation is the Edo cargo tradition; the bezaisen type itself
  postdates 1597, and source 1's own sentence implies a time before the machine came
  aboard even there.
- **Class**: cargo to war, with NO textual bridge. r174 shipped a fleet-class extension
  on the Tiangong Kaiwu's explicit 器具皆同 ("the gear all the same"); r175 shipped a
  navy-tradition extension on the navy's own album. Nothing equivalent exists here —
  and source 4 says the class's contemporary record could not carry such a bridge.
- **Placement**: the attested stand is inside an aft WORKING house, spindle between
  floor and overhead beam. The sekibune's aft house is the commander's yakata; her open
  yagura deck offers no head bearing. Any drawn placement would be invented.

Mechanically the extension is also unneeded: the machine let a cargo crew of a dozen
manage a heavy yard; this warship records ~70 aboard (that a warship's hands hoisted by
direct pull is itself an inference, and is marked as one in the record).

**The record therefore stays silent and the builder draws nothing.** The judgment lives
in the data: hull.windlassProvenance (JUDGED SILENT + reasons) and a rows entry on the
card, so a reader sees the question was asked and answered rather than never asked.
Scope: the judgment is for the drawn 1597 instance. The class ran to 1868; an Edo
sekibune shares the attested machine's own era, and a drawn Edo instance would reopen
the question — stated in the provenance.
