## The form, resolved against the report's own complete-form schematic (p. 205)

The schematic (완형 추정 모식도, for 마도해역-203) draws: two cheek timbers,
seat faces inward; cross battens tenoned through the square mortises; the rope
descending between the horns and bending on a batten; the stone through the
gap near the butt, its ends projecting both sides; the fluke ends sweeping
OUTWARD (away from the partner) into curved points. Assembly geometry that
falls out of the 212 arm's own cuts:

- the two seat faces sit the stone's THICKNESS apart (0.10 m) — the stone's
  broad faces are what the cheeks clamp, and Xu Jing's clause lands exactly:
  石兩旁夾以二木鈎, on the stone's two sides it is clamped by the two wooden
  hooks;
- the stone's WIDTH (0.29) lies along the cheek run in the 0.25 m waist seats;
- the stone's LENGTH (1.09) crosses perpendicular to the fluke plane,
  projecting ~0.46 m each side — the stone is the STOCK as well as the
  ballast, forcing the fluke plane vertical on the seabed, precisely the
  admiralty-stock geometry, at the crown;
- the battens splay-pin the frame at 0.58/0.90/1.37; the cable bends to the
  0.90 batten (the schematic's own wrap) and leads out between the horns.

SCALE IS CONTESTED and the provenance says so: 84 kg reads as a coastal
trader's anchor, and Xu Jing's kezhou (a four-man windlass, a rafter-thick
500-chi cable) likely swung heavier — the corpus's ceiling is 마도해역-203
(98×40×34, 255 kg, raised with one arm and a bamboo rope the report reads as
Chinese practice; = the 2023 report's stone 052) and 마도해역-206 (178×28×13,
133 kg, an 18 cm 결구부 seat and retaining grooves cut mid-length). But no
larger pair survives measured, and a scaled-up 212 would be a derivation with
an invented factor. The drawn object is the excavated pair at its own size —
the only find in the tradition where arm and stone are one object, raised from
the Mado anchorage on the very Goryeo west-coast route Xu Jing's fleet sailed
in 1123.

## Stow

r183's stow holds (recovered, laid on the open foredeck, head aft, cable to
the barrel) with one addition the new form forces: the assembly is cruciform
in section (horns ±Z, stone ±X), so flat-on-deck is impossible — a rigid
cruciform on a plane comes to rest rolled onto two adjacent limbs. Drawn at
repose: rolled ~50° about its own shank, one stone end and one horn low,
settled to the deck by measurement (the r183/r185 rule). Iterated by looking.
