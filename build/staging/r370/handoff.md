
## Round 370 — 2026-11-27 — The 74's stowed bower now rests its bill on Steel's bill-board, above the fore channel, and her side carries his anchor-lining and its bolster under the channel. r369 asked whether the anchor should lie clear of the upper-deck ports, or whether a 74 really fouled a port with her stowed anchor. Steel answers it in the passage r366 already quoted, read to its end: "The bill-board may then be carried upwards from the upperside of the channel to the top of the side. The anchor lining commences at the upperside of the bolster, which, in the eighty-gun ship, may be well with the upperside of the channel-wale". The channel-wale is the wale under the upper-deck ports, so the lining covers the whole height of the upper-deck port band, from the channel-wale up to the channel. In Steel's own ship the fished anchor lies across that band, and it cannot be lifted clear of it. What the model had wrong was the bill's resting place. The solver laid the bill under the channel, against the lining, which is where it drags while the anchor is being fished (Steel's glossary: the lining is there "when fishing the anchor"). Falconer (1769, FISH) has the fish "hoist or draw up the flukes of the ship's anchor towards the top of the bow in order to stow it". So the stowed bill rests on the bill-board, over the channel. The lining and the bill-board were not drawn before this round.
