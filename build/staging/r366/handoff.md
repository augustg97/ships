**THE SOURCES.**
- **Falconer, *Universal Dictionary of the Marine*, ANCHOR** (`build/staging/r172/falconer.txt` lines 1170–1186): the arms are "of equal thickness with the beam (usually called the shank) only that they taper towards the points, which are elevated above the horizontal plane at an angle of thirty degrees; or inclined to the shank at an angle of sixty degrees: on the upper part of each arm (in this position) is a fluke, or thick plate of iron, commonly shaped like an isosceles triangle, whose base reaches inwards to the middle of the arm. On the upper-end of the shank is fixed the stock transversely with the flukes: the stock is a long beam of oak, in two parts, strongly bolted, and hooped together with iron rings ... Close above the stock is the ring." Falconer's SHANK-PAINTER: "a short rope and chain which hangs the shank and flukes of the anchor up to the ship's side".
- **Steel, *Elements and Practice of Naval Architecture* (1822), Book II on the sheer draught** (`build/staging/r356/dl/steel1822.txt` line 15775): "let us see that the fore channel is long enough to take the anchor-lining and bill-board for stowing the anchor, thus: get the length of the anchor to the bill, and allow for the cat-block; then sweep the distance so obtained from the cat-head aft, and the curve that the bill of the anchor is supposed to make will give the middle of the lining". His glossary (line 1084): the anchor-lining is "short pieces of plank, or of board, fastened to the sides of the ship, or to stantions under the fore channel, to prevent the bill of the anchor from wounding the ship's side, when fishing the anchor".
- Photographs looked at and not measured: two Commons photographs of HMS Victory's bow (`dl/v-b71f3c.jpg`, `dl/v-0c7a9d.jpg`). The museum anchor on the quay shows the arms rising from the crown toward the ring.

**AUDIT.** **New rule A-ANCHOR-FORM**, every anchor: the shank is the longest cylinder, the ring the torus, the crown the shank's end away from the ring. Every other cylinder with 12 or more sides is an arm, and the angle at the crown between its farthest vertex and the ring must be 60° within 5°. **New rule A-ANCHOR-CLEAR**, every hull with a cathead: (a) no anchor vertex inside the skin at its own station and height; (b) no lower shroud within 0.05 m of an anchor vertex at that vertex's height; (c) an anchor vertex within 0.5 m of the skin (a bill on the side). The three bows without a cathead are left out, and the rule's comment says so. **The round-99 rule "anchor fork athwart the ship" is retired**: it compared the two cone flukes' distances off the centreline, and r366 has no cones and tilts the fork on purpose. A-ANCHOR-CLEAR (a) and (c) convict both faults it was written for.
