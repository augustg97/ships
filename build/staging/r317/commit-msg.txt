Round 317: the East Indiaman (1620) and the slave ship (1590) set a lateen on the mizzen, under the mizzen topsail, as a ship of their period did (Waters 1958: "the mizzen mast carried a lateen-sail. This rig remained typical of merchant ships well into the eighteenth" century; Falconer 1769, MIZEN). With the crossjack bare since r315 they had set nothing there. A square mast's record now takes `lateenMizzen`, and the builder slings the lateen yard, parrel and cloth to the square mast's own lower section. The stage-7 card names a square mizzen's lateen or spanker; it had said "square sails on all three masts" on the 74, the clipper and Preussen, which set a spanker. The furled card no longer says a lateen yard is lowered, since the builder keeps it aloft. A-MIZZEN-SAIL convicts a square mizzen with no fore-and-aft sail, a sail out of its period, an unsourced or unbuilt lateen, and a sling over the crossjack. The lateen read now takes each vertex once: a cylinder's repeated seam vertex had read a yard on the square mast as through it. The frames are scored in the receipt.
