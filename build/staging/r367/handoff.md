**FOR r368:**
1. **Preussen's stock stands square off her side.** Her stow solves at 85°, the first clear angle from flat-on-the-side, because the cathead's class length (0.26 B, 4.3 m) puts its tip far out from a fine steel bow. From the bow quarter the stock reads as a spar sticking out of the forecastle. Find a plate of a Laeisz ship's bow at sea (Passat and Peking survive) and read the cathead's length and the stow. If she housed stockless anchors in hawse pipes, model that form.
2. **The stock's material by era.** r366 made every stock oak, after Falconer (1769). Admiralty-pattern bowers of the iron-ship era had iron stocks, keyed so they could be drawn and laid along the shank. The steamer and Preussen draw oak stocks with hoops. The corbita's anchor form is also contested: Roman anchors had wooden stocks with lead, or a movable iron stock (the Nemi anchors).
