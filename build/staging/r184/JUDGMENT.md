The section, verbatim (Gutenberg text, traditional characters):

> 凡舟行遇風難泊，則全身系命於錨。戰舡、海舡，有重千鈞者。錘法：先成四爪，
> 以次逐節接身。其三百斤以內者，用徑尺闊砧，安頓爐傍，當其兩端皆紅，掀去爐炭，
> 鐵包木棍，夾持上砧。若千斤內外者，則架木為棚，多人立其上，共持鐵鏈，兩接錨身，
> 其末皆帶巨鐵圈鏈套，提起捩轉，咸力錘合。合藥不用黃泥，先取陳久壁土篩細，
> 一人頻撒介面之中，渾合方無微罅。蓋爐錘之中，此物其最巨者。

Read: when a ship under way meets wind and cannot moor, the whole ship's lives
hang on the anchor; war-ships and sea-ships carry anchors up to a thousand jun.
**The forging method: FIRST MAKE THE FOUR CLAWS (先成四爪), then join them
section by section to the body (以次逐節接身).** Those within 300 catties are
forged on a foot-wide anvil beside the furnace; those around a thousand catties
on a timber stage, many men holding chain slings to turn the work under the
hammers. The weld flux is sifted aged wall-earth. Of everything under furnace
and hammer, the anchor is the largest.

So the form is settled by the primary text: the Chinese sea anchor of this
tradition is the **four-claw iron anchor** (四爪錨) — four claws forge-welded
to a shank. No stock, no wooden parts: whichever way it lands, a claw points
down and bites (the grapnel's own geometry, in iron throughout).

The 雲車 windlass winds the cable to break the anchor out — the machine this
hull already carries (r174).

## Standings, every one named

- Form (four claws to a shank): RECORDED — 錘鍛 verbatim, fetched this round.
- Sheet-anchor weight 500 catties: RECORDED (重五百斤內外). At the Ming catty
  ≈ 0.59–0.60 kg, ~295–300 kg.
- Sheet-anchor DIMENSIONS: DERIVED CLASS DEFAULT — no dimension is in any
  text. Shank 2.4 m × 0.11 m dia + four claws ~1.0 m at wrought iron
  ~7,800 kg/m³ sums to ≈ 300 kg, the recorded weight. Named as derived.
- Head-pair weight: NOT RECORDED (其餘 — "the rest", no number). CLASS DEFAULT
  300 catties, the 錘鍛 text's own anvil/stage threshold (其三百斤以內者 —
  the ordinary-forge class boundary), named as an INFERENCE with that hook.
  Linear scale (300/500)^(1/3) ≈ 0.84 → shank 2.0 m.
- Claw length ~0.42 × shank: CLASS DEFAULT off the TGK 錘鍛 woodcut's
  proportions, read as form only, no scale bar claimed (the plate is a
  woodcut, not a measured drawing — the Azzam rule cuts both ways).
- Stow stations: the HEAD is the record's (頭用兩枝, the posts and the winch
  both at the bow); the exact spots on the foredeck are PLACEMENT INFERENCE,
  named, iterated by measure_ship and looked at (the r182/r183 method).
- NOT fetched, named: the Nanjing/Jinghai temple anchor traditions and any
  measured surviving 四爪錨 (Quanzhou museum holdings); a dimensioned find
  would replace the derived defaults and the provenance says so.

  point ON the deck at its own station, asked of the surface); V-CABLE (a
  drawn anchor with no cable convicts — the 舟車 sentence carries anchor,
  cable, posts and winch together).
