# Ellmers, "The Hanseatic Cog of Bremen AD 1380", Drassana (Museu Marítim, Barcelona) —
# fetched 2026-08-31 (r173) from raco.cat (PDF, 15 pp). Author was the DSM's first director.

The two sentences the r173 judgment stands on, verbatim:

> "The upper gallery allows to look from above onto the deck and the aftcastle with a
> windlass in its middle and a capstan on its top."

> "But when the man at the tiller had to stand underneath the castle-deck in between the
> long cabins at both sides and behind the heavy windlass, he could no longer look around
> the sea nor could he even see his own sail. From this postion the skiper could not guide
> his ship any longer. For the first time he had to delegate some aspects of his
> responsibilities. He found his new position on the deck of the aftcastle ..."

Context: the ship described is the reconstructed Bremen cog in the cog hall (reassembled
by Werner Lahn's team 1972–78; published documentation Lahn 1992, *Die Kogge von Bremen*,
Schriften des DSM Nr. 30, with 36 plan- and detail-drawings — "the base for all the models
... as well as for the replicas in original size").

Also relevant (hull data, matching the vessel record): LOA 23.23 m, breadth 7.62 m, keel
15.6 m, midships keel-to-washstrake 4.26 m, ~60 t light, 44 herring lasts (~84 t) at 2.25 m
draught, one square sail ~200 m². Crew 11 per the 1403/04 documents. The cog was unfinished
when she sank: the two upper gudgeons were never nailed on, so the rudder had not been hung.
